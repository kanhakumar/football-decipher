# Decisions

Short ADR-style log. Newest at the bottom.

## ADR-001: Toolchain (accepted, 2026-10-20)
JDK 25 (Temurin LTS), Scala 3.9 LTS, sbt 1.13, Python 3.13. Latest stable lines, not the newest releases: Scala Next (3.10) and sbt 2.x are skipped because they trade stability for features we do not need this week.

## ADR-002: Effect stack is cats-effect + fs2 + http4s + circe (accepted, 2026-10-20)
The core is a replay and streaming engine, so fs2 `Stream` is the natural model for the generator, the feed emulator (jitter, out-of-order delivery and dropouts via `metered`, `evalMap` and `delayBy`) and the roughly 10 s viewer-delayed fan-out (`Topic`, `broadcastThrough`), with backpressure included. http4s has first-class server-sent events, and circe's explicit codecs suit golden-file testing. Pure core with effects at the edges maps onto `IO`. ZIO would also work; we pick cats-effect and fs2 for streaming ergonomics, the SSE and HTTP stack, and munit-cats-effect. Never mix the two stacks. No Akka.

Versions: cats-effect 3.7.1, fs2 3.14.0, http4s 0.23.38 (the 1.0 line is still milestone-only), circe 0.14.17.

## ADR-003: Scala models are hand-written, pinned by golden tests (accepted, 2026-10-20)
No codegen on the Scala side. The canonical event schema selects the `details` shape, the allowed `outcome` values and the required fields with `if/then/else` keyed on `type`; no maintained Scala 3 generator expresses that, so output would be hand-edited anyway. Hand-written sealed ADTs also give exhaustive matching in detectors.

Sync mechanism, with `/schema` as the source of truth:
1. Every example is validated against the schemas on the JVM (`SchemaConformanceSuite`, the twin of `schema/validate_examples.py`), including known-bad variants.
2. Golden round trip: decode, re-encode, compare (`EnvelopeGoldenSuite`; extended to all models in step 2).
3. From step 3, everything the generator emits is validated against the schema in tests.

Python keeps datamodel-code-generator for its models. Test-only dependency: `com.networknt:json-schema-validator` 3.0.8 (draft 2020-12, format assertions switched on).

Step 2 outcome: all payloads are typed (`smi.model`). Decoders enforce the schema, not just the shape: unknown fields are rejected, IDs and timestamps are refined opaque types, ranges and per-type rules (required fields, allowed outcomes, the `details` shape chosen by event `type`) are checked, and absent optionals are omitted on encode. Timestamps keep their original string so re-encoding is byte-identical. Known-bad variants live in one data file, `schema/negatives.json`, which both `validate_examples.py` and the Scala golden tests apply, so the schema and the decoders must reject the same cases.

## ADR-004: Server-sent events as the stream transport (accepted, 2026-10-20)
`GET /events/stream` emits `text/event-stream`; `id` is the envelope `seq` (supports resume), `event` is the payload type, `data` is the envelope JSON. Chosen over WebSockets because the viewer only receives, browsers reconnect with `Last-Event-ID` for free, and it works through standard HTTP infrastructure. Spike status: served the first `event` envelope of the examples file. Superseded by the full API in ADR-013 (`/api/stream/events`).

## ADR-005: `match_clock_s` is total elapsed playing time (accepted, 2026-10-20)
`match_clock_s` = playing time of all earlier periods (including their stoppage time) + `period_clock_s`. It is monotonic across halves, as the invariants require. The broadcast-style display ("45+2'", second half restarting at 45:00) is derived from `period` and `period_clock_s` at render time, never stored.

## ADR-006: Generator v0 design (accepted, 2026-10-20)
- One SplitMix64 RNG per match, seeded from the CLI, is the only randomness. All maths goes through `StrictMath` so the same seed is byte-identical on any JVM or CPU. Output values are rounded (coordinates 0.1 m, clocks 0.1 s) before they are written or reused.
- Pure from the outside: `MatchGenerator.generate(config)` returns the envelopes; mutable state lives inside one private simulation instance.
- A possession loop: the ball carrier shoots (chance by 12 x 8 grid cell) or passes to a teammate chosen by distance and forwardness from a 4-3-3 shape that shifts with the ball. Pass risk depends on length and destination zone. Failures become interceptions, loose balls, throw-ins or offside free kicks; shots end in goals, saves (keeper or corner), blocks or goal kicks.
- Fixed default kickoff time, so output never depends on the current date. `video_ts_ms` = wall time since kickoff + 10 s pre-roll.
- v0 has no per-player attributes; when step 4 adds them they go in a generator-only file the pipeline never reads.

## ADR-007: Scenario control with styles, phases and soft beats (accepted, 2026-10-20)
A scenario YAML (default: `core/src/main/resources/scenarios/default.yaml`, override with `generate --scenario <file>`) scripts a match without scripting events. Generator-only: the pipeline never reads it.
- Team styles: `press`, `directness`, `tempo`, `aggression` (roughly 0..1).
- Phases override one team's knobs between two display minutes (default: home high press 55-70).
- Beats (`counter_goal`, `red_card`) are soft. Around the target minute the relevant odds are multiplied by a boost that ramps up over the 5 minutes before, holds for 4 minutes after and fades by +6; once the beat happens it closes. A counter-goal boost applies only while the possession still qualifies as a counter (ball won in own half by tackle or interception, at most 15 s and 5 passes), so every boosted goal is a genuine counter. Nothing is forced: the control scenario without beats produces neither story at that rate.
- Minutes are display minutes (34 = first half at 34:00, 78 = second half at 33:00).
- A red-card beat also makes the team hunt the ball (more pressure and challenges), since a red needs fouls and fouls need defending.
- Measured on seeds 1-50 after calibration (step 5): counter goal within 5 minutes of target in 47/50, red card in 50/50, home pressures per away pass 1.52x higher during the press phase. Without beats: 0/50 and 2/50. Beats are soft, so any single seed may miss one (seed 42 has no counter goal); pick the demo seed by checking its summary.
- Dependency: `io.circe::circe-yaml-v12` 1.15.0, decoded into strict case classes with the shared codec setup.

## ADR-008: Calibration gates the unscripted baseline over 200 seeds (accepted, 2026-10-20)
- `sbt calibrate` measures seeds 1-200 and writes `docs/calibration.md` with `--report`. `CalibrationSuite` runs the same gate in `sbt test`.
- The gate is the **baseline**: the default scenario's styles and phase with its beats removed. The full story scenario is reported next to it. Its scripted counter goal and red card add about one goal and one red card per match by design; the test checks the story stays in range on every other metric and adds 0.5 to 1.5 goals.
- 200 seeds, not 50: goals per match vary by about 1.6, so a 50-seed average moves by about 0.2, almost half the 2.5-3.0 band. 200 seeds halve that.
- Target bands from the project brief: goals 2.5-3, shots 20-28, pass completion 78-90%, events 1,500-2,500 per match, possession 35-65% in every match (plus a 45-55% average). Plausibility bands chosen for this project, not taken from any data source: shots on target 7-10, passes per team 350-550, fouls 18-26.
- Possession is measured as the share of passes. Shots on target are goals plus saves.
- Main tuning changes: higher shot rates per grid cell, lower finishing (`0.46 e^(-d/7) + 0.02`), slower ball holding, more fouls, defenders on a yellow foul less, home slightly more direct (0.45).

## ADR-009: Provider-style raw feed, adapter and feed emulator (accepted, 2026-10-20)
The pipeline must show a path to real provider data without using any. "synthfeed" is our own provider-style format; nothing in it comes from vendor documentation or ID tables.
- Format (`smi.raw`, registry in `core/src/main/resources/raw/registry.yaml`): messages of kind `match`, `lineup` or `event` with action `upsert` or `delete` and a `last_modified` time. Events carry numeric type IDs, provider team and player IDs, period + minute + second (the second half starts at minute 45), a wall-clock `timestamp`, 0..100 coordinates (team-relative), an outcome bit and numbered qualifiers with string values. All IDs are our own sequential numbering.
- Precision: raw coordinates have 2 decimals and the adapter keeps 4 decimals in metres, so raw -> canonical -> raw is exact. Canonical -> raw -> canonical moves coordinates by at most about 5 mm and drops tenths of a second (the raw clock is whole seconds). `video_ts_ms` comes from `timestamp - kickoff + video_offset_ms` and is exact.
- IDs: canonical IDs are derived (`E_<raw id>`, team from the registry, `P_<team suffix>_<shirt>` from the lineup), and every canonical event carries `source_ids` back to the raw record.
- Outcomes: the bit says complete, goal or won; a `result` qualifier carries anything the bit cannot (for example `intercepted` or `saved`).
- Adapter (`Adapter`, immutable state): match and lineup messages publish at once; event upserts wait in a reorder buffer (10 s of event time) and publish in time order; an upsert for a published event is an `update`, a delete is a `delete`. Repairs and rejections are reported as issues, never silent: a missing `result` qualifier gets the type's default outcome, coordinates up to 1 unit outside 0..100 are clamped, anything unknown or missing a required field is rejected.
- Emulator (`FeedEmulator`, seeded): latency and jitter, about 2% of events delayed by 2-7 s (inside the reorder window, so recoverable), 3 corrections a minute or more later, 2 phantom events that are later deleted, and one 20-40 s dropout released as a burst. `FeedEmulator.stream` replays a schedule at 1x to 20x.
- Recovery check: the adapter's final state from the messy deliveries equals its state from the clean feed with corrections applied (`FeedEmulator.truth`).

## ADR-010: Stats engine (accepted, 2026-10-20)
`smi.engine` turns canonical envelopes into facts. It is pure, reads only canonical data, and never imports generator code (a test enforces this).
- State: an event store by ID; per-minute buckets keyed by (period, minute of period) and by team or player, holding every additive metric. Creates add an event's contributions, updates subtract the old version and add the new one (possibly in another minute), deletes subtract. Order-dependent state (score, players on the pitch, cards, possession) is rebuilt from the store when first asked for. A property test checks that any mix of corrections, deletes and restores equals a full recompute.
- Enrichment (raw events are never changed): engine-owned zones (thirds at 35 and 70 m, penalty box, zone 14, an 18-zone grid); xG from distance and goal-mouth angle (logistic, hand-set: about 0.27 from 6 m central, 0.11 from the penalty spot, 0.03 from 20 m; headers x0.6, direct free kicks x0.8, penalties 0.76), set so mean xG tracks mean goals in the synthetic matches (3.22 vs 2.82 over 50 unscripted seeds) and not fitted to any data; expected pass completion from length, forwardness, end zone, height and pressure; progressive pass = completed and at least max(10 m, 25% of the distance to goal) closer; final-third and box entries.
- Metric registry: each metric declares unit, calculation (count, sum, or ratio of two at query time), the capability level it requires, a fallback and its basis. Distance covered and sprints need tracking (level 1); until tracking exists they answer with involvements, tagged `event_estimate` and naming the fallback. Every result is a `MetricValue` with value, unit, basis and capability level; undefined ratios are empty, never zero.
- Definitions: possession share = share of passes; field tilt = share of final-third passes; PPDA = opponent passes in their own 60% divided by our tackles, interceptions and fouls in that area.
- Evidence: the same predicate that counts a metric lists the events behind it, for `evidence_event_ids` in step 8.

## ADR-011: Moment detectors and re-derivable insights (accepted, 2026-10-20)
`smi.insight` turns engine state into Insight objects. It reads the engine and canonical models only, never generator code.
- Contract: a detector `find`s a moment (evidence event IDs plus a header: kind, period, teams, players, window bounds), then `derive`s every fact from the evidence events alone, and computes `why_it_matters` and `importance` from the facts alone. Window bounds are stored in the facts, so `Rederive.check` can rebuild the header from a published insight and must reproduce facts, reason and importance exactly. Every number in `params` is also a fact, so the Verifier only has to check narration against facts.
- Runner (`Moments`): replays the feed through the engine; event detectors run on each new event, window detectors when a minute completes; 10-minute cooldown per detector and team. Times come from the triggering event, `created_at` from its wall clock (deterministic).
- Corrections: an update or delete to an evidence or trigger event re-checks the insight. Same facts: nothing. Different facts: the old insight is re-emitted `retracted` and a replacement with `supersedes` is published. No longer true: retracted only. An update can also create a new insight (a saved shot corrected to a goal).
- Detectors and thresholds: goal (scoreline codes, or `counter_attack_goal` when the possession began with a tackle or interception in the team's own half and the shot came within 15 s and 5 completed passes, computed from events, not the shot's situation tag); red card; key chance (unscored, xG at least 0.25); momentum swing (attacking-action share from 40% or less to 60% or more, 10-minute windows); pressure shift (pressures per opponent pass over 6 minutes at least 1.5x the previous 8 to 15 minutes, at least 10 pressures); control vs chaos (turnovers up 35% to 26 or more in 10 minutes, or one team's pass share from 55% or less to 65% or more); rhythm change (passes per minute changes 35% or more); milestones (50, 75, 100 completed passes; 10 and 20 team shots; brace, hat-trick).
- Measured on 50 seeds: about 27 insights per match; every generator counter goal is detected as one and nothing else is (two independent implementations agree); every red card detected; the home high press found in 41/50 seeds. Without beats: counter story 0/50, red card story 2/50.
- Demo seed 154 (default scenario) shows all three scripted moments with a close scoreline.

## ADR-012: Synthetic 5 Hz tracking and workload metrics (accepted, 2026-10-21)
- Source: `smi.tracking.TrackingSynth` builds frames from a canonical feed (lineups and events) and a seed. It is data-source code like the generator; the engine and detectors never import it (tested). `generate --tracking` merges frames into the feed by wall-clock time and renumbers `seq` (event IDs unchanged); about 29,500 frames and 75 MB per match, so it is off by default.
- Frame convention: coordinates in the home team's frame (home attacks toward x = 105 all match), within the schema's tracking bounds.
- Movement: each player heads for their lineup slot, shifted with the ball and by line height (5 m up in possession, 5 m back out of it), with slow seeded wander; event actors are pulled to the event spot from 6 s before, pass receivers to the pass end. Recovery runs at top speed when more than 10 m from the shape. Limits: top speed by role (about 7 m/s keeper, 8.5 defence, 9 midfield, 9.5 attack, with up to 4% seeded variation), acceleration 3.5 m/s2, and an approach speed the player can still stop from (no overshoot). Fatigue lowers speed and acceleration with time and high-speed running; half-time recovers some; substitutes come on fresh. Only players on the pitch appear. The ball sits at each event spot at event time and travels to the pass end or next event; officials follow simple speed-limited rules.
- Measured on seed 154: acting players within 3 m of the event spot for 98.7% of events; full-match outfield players about 11.5 to 12 km, keepers about 5.6 km, top speeds 28 to 34 km/h, about 20 sprints per full-match outfield player, and lower distance per minute late than early.
- Engine workload: frames are folded into per-minute buckets per player and team, never stored. Distance between consecutive frames (gaps over 1 s ignored), high-speed distance above 5.5 m/s, top speed, sprints (7 m/s or more held for 1 s), and a fatigue index (distance per minute in a window as a percentage of the holder's own first 15 minutes). Capability becomes 1 once frames arrive.
- Degrading: a window answers from tracking (basis `tracking`, capability 1) only if at least 90% of the expected frames arrived; otherwise distance and sprints fall back to involvements (basis `event_estimate`) and the other tracking metrics report not available. `PositionProvider` returns a position or `NotAvailable` with a reason (no tracking data, not on the pitch, stale for more than 1 s).
- `Rng` moved to the shared `smi` package so the generator, feed emulator and tracking synthesizer share it without depending on each other.

## ADR-013: Core HTTP and SSE API (accepted, 2026-10-21)
- One replayed match at a time in an in-memory hub (`smi.api.Hub`, a cats-effect `SignallingRef`). A ticker advances a playhead by real time times speed (0.25x to 600x; above 20x is for tests and rehearsals). Two cursors: the live cursor feeds the engine and detectors at the generator's position; the viewer cursor trails it by `delay_s` of match time (default 10 s, D5). Agents work on the live side, so they can narrate before the viewer sees the moment.
- Control: `POST /api/match/start` (seed, speed, delay_s, tracking), `pause`, `resume`, `speed`; `GET /api/match/state`. Restarting ends the previous match's streams.
- Streams (SSE): `/api/stream/events` (viewer-delayed envelopes, id = index = seq), `/api/stream/insights` (every insight version, live side), `/api/stream/overlays`. Each replays retained history from `from` or after `Last-Event-ID`, then follows the match, and ends when it finishes.
- Overlays: the agent service publishes verified overlays with `POST /api/overlays` (strictly decoded, so schema-valid; one publish per ID and op; must match the current match). The core itself publishes only factual template overlays: a scoreline update per goal insight, retracted if the goal insight is retracted. Core overlay IDs start at O_90000000.
- Tool endpoints for the MCP server (`/api/tools/...`): match state, team and player stats over a window (explicit minutes or `last_minutes`), player workload, team shape (average positions: tracking when covered, else event locations as an estimate), recent events (at most 50, compact), insight detail (latest version plus an evidence summary with at most 20 sample events). Every number carries unit, basis and capability level; undefined values are omitted. They read the live engine.
- OpenAPI: hand-written `core/src/main/resources/openapi.yaml`, served at `/openapi.yaml`; a test checks it documents exactly the routes the server has. No new dependencies.
- CORS allows any origin, because the viewer is served from another origin. Tighten when deployed.


## ADR-014: Agent workflow, MCP server and Verifier (accepted, 2026-10-21)
- Dependencies (Python, `agents/pyproject.toml`): `agent-framework-core` 1.20.0 (workflow graph only, not the `[all]` meta-package), `mcp` 2.3.0 (official SDK v2: `MCPServer`, `Client`), `httpx` 0.28.1, `pydantic` 2, `pyyaml` 6; dev: `datamodel-code-generator` 0.83.0. Async tests use the anyio pytest plugin that ships with mcp. The Foundry client package is added at the Foundry swap.
- MCP server (`smi_agents.mcp_server`): one tool per core tool endpoint (match_state, match_info, team_stats, player_stats, player_workload, team_shape, recent_events, list_insights, get_insight), typed arguments, the core's JSON returned unchanged, core 4xx as tool errors. stdio by default, streamable HTTP with `--http`. The core gained `GET /api/tools/match_info` (team names and squads) so lines can name teams and players.
- Facts through MCP only: a fixed tool plan per insight kind, chosen by code, not by the model (get_insight and match_info always; team_stats over the spell for pressure, momentum, control and rhythm insights). The result is a FactSheet: the only numbers, names and event claims a line may use. Nothing bulk reaches a prompt.
- Workflow (Agent Framework `WorkflowBuilder` graph): Gather, Explain (model), Personalize (code: tone, perspective, focus), Narrate (model, English), Verify (code), Localize (model, hi/es) and Verify again, then Publish. Conditional edges: a rejected line goes back to the Narrator or Localizer with the reasons, up to 3 attempts per stage; then Template. An unparseable model reply counts as a rejection. The output is a Narration (line, verified, template_fallback, attempt trail), turned into an `insight_card` overlay timed by the insight's `video_ts_ms`. Cached by request (insight, persona, language, favourites, focus), per D3.
- Verifier rules: every number (digits, Devanagari digits, number words two and up in en/es/hi) must be a correct rounding of a FactSheet number, a share shown as a percentage, or an evidence minute (minute marks must be minutes). Metric cards must match a fact. Names from the squads must belong to the insight or its evidence. A per-language lexicon of event claims (penalty, red card, equaliser, lead, counter, save, result and more) each needs support from the insight kind, reason code, evidence event types or fact flags. Plus schema lengths, no raw ids, Hindi lines in Devanagari.
- Templates (`templates.yaml`): one line per reason code (all 20 the detectors emit) in en, hi and es, with persona framing (analyst basis line, club fan for/against by the code's polarity, player focus) and metric cards by persona. They are the fallback and the stub model's output, and every template line for every demo insight is tested to pass the Verifier.
- Model seam: `Model.complete(task, system, payload) -> JSON text`. `StubModel` (deterministic, offline), `ScriptedModel` (queued replies for tests), `ChatClientModel` (any Agent Framework chat client; used for Foundry later). Prompts in `smi_agents/prompts/`.
- Python models for insight and overlay are generated from /schema (`agents/scripts/gen_models.py`); a test fails when they are stale. Tests run offline against responses recorded from the core for seed 154 (`agents/scripts/record_fixtures.py`).
- `python -m smi_agents follow --publish` narrates insights at or above an importance threshold as they stream (D4 moment path), posts overlays (IDs from O_00000001) and retracts them when the insight is retracted or replaced.

## ADR-015: Viewer and on-demand narration (accepted, 2026-10-21)
- The viewer (`/viewer`) is a static page in plain HTML, CSS and ES modules: no build step, no dependencies, served by any static server now and by Static Web Apps later. Fictional branding only ("Football Decipher Live").
- It reads three core streams: the viewer-delayed feed (clock, pitch, ticker, tracking dots), insights from the live side, and core overlays (scoreline updates and retractions). It computes no match facts: the score comes from the goal insights' facts, momentum and player panels from core tool endpoints, cards from verified narrations.
- Narration is on demand and per viewer (D3): for each insight at or above an importance threshold (0.6 by default), the viewer asks the agent service (`python -m smi_agents serve`, `POST /api/narrate`) for its persona, language, favourite club, favourite player and metric focus. Identical requests share one workflow run and the cache. Changing a setting re-narrates every moment in place, including the card on screen. Agent overlays that `follow --publish` posts to the core are not shown again by the viewer.
- Scheduling (D5, D8): a viewer clock follows the newest envelope's `video_ts_ms` and extrapolates at playback speed (capped at 8 s of video). A card shows when the clock reaches its `t_start_ms`, one per slot (lower third, corner), for its `duration_ms` and at least 4.5 s of wall time so it can be read at fast playback; a card whose window has passed is skipped. The moments list never shows a moment before the viewer reaches it.
- Retract path: a retracted or superseded insight withdraws its card (queued or on screen; on screen it is struck through with a correction banner for 4 s) and is marked withdrawn in the moments list; a retract overlay from the core does the same; the score follows the remaining goal insights.
- Latency readout: viewer delay (video and real seconds), narration round trip, how far ahead of its moment a narration was ready, and whether the last card showed on time (in wall time). At fast replays the delay must cover narration in real time: delay_s / speed (the Start form has a Delay field; 60 s at 20x gives 3 s). The agent service warms up the workflow runtime at start-up so the first moment is not slow.
- The agent service is Starlette on uvicorn (both already installed with the MCP SDK), with CORS open for the static viewer; tighten when deployed. `NarrationRequest.match_id` lets the service refuse a request for a match the core no longer plays (409).
- Viewer logic (clock, overlay queue, score, coordinates, insight book) lives in pure modules tested with `node --test` (Node 18+, no packages).

## ADR-016: Feed sources, cloud topology and the eval gate (accepted, 2026-10-21)
- Feed source: the hub plays a `Replay` (the whole feed generated up front and released by the playhead, ADR-013; the local default) or a `Bus` (envelopes arrive one by one, as from a live provider). On a bus the live cursor processes each envelope as it lands, the viewer cursor trails by `delay_s` of video time but never passes what has arrived, and the match ends on the run's last envelope; `envelopes_total` is unknown until then. A bus match gives exactly the same feed, insights and overlays as a replay (tested).
- `MatchBus`: `InMemoryBus` (tests, `SMI_FEED=memory`) and `EventHubsBus` (`SMI_FEED=eventhubs`): one hub `match-feed` with one partition so a run keeps its order; each event is one canonical envelope as JSON, with run id, last marker and speed as properties; the consumer reads from the latest position. In bus mode `/api/match/start` starts an in-process publisher (the generated feed at playback pace; pause and speed act on it). The generator job (`smi.Publish`) is the same publisher run standalone; the core picks up any run that starts with match meta, and drops envelopes of a replaced run.
- `MatchStore`: insights and overlays appended per run (`InMemoryStore` in tests, `CosmosStore` when `SMI_COSMOS_ENDPOINT` is set: serverless NoSQL, database `fd`, containers `insights` and `overlays` by `/match_id`; the agents keep `narrations` by `/insight_id`). Writes happen off the ticker's path; a failed write is logged and does not stop the match.
- Azure topology (East US 2, `azd up`, Bicep in /infra): Container Apps for `core` (1 replica: the hub is in memory) and `agents` (1 replica), a manual Container Apps job `generator`, Event Hubs (Basic), Cosmos DB (serverless), Foundry (AI Services account, project, gpt-5-mini Global Standard, 100K TPM of the 500K quota), Static Web Apps (Free) for the viewer, Container Registry (Basic, images built remotely, no local Docker), Key Vault (the Application Insights connection string), Log Analytics and Application Insights. One user-assigned managed identity with only the roles it needs (Event Hubs sender and receiver, Cosmos data contributor, Cognitive Services OpenAI user, Azure AI user, AcrPull, Key Vault secrets user); local auth off where the service allows; no keys or secrets in the repo or app settings. CORS allows the Static Web App origin only. Tracing: the Application Insights Java agent for the core (no code), Agent Framework's OpenTelemetry for the agents.
- Core packaging: `sbt assembly` (sbt-assembly 2.5.0) and `core/Dockerfile` on eclipse-temurin 25 JRE. New core dependencies: azure-messaging-eventhubs 5.21.7, azure-cosmos 4.83.0, azure-identity 1.18.7, slf4j-simple 2.0.20 (warnings and errors from http4s and the Azure SDK now reach stderr).
- Eval gate (`eval/run.py`): narrates every insight at or above 0.6 importance of the demo match (seed 154) in 5 personas and 3 languages and scores number match (an independent checker on the raw tool answers, not the Verifier; must be 100%), verified rate, template-fallback rate, first-attempt rate and p95 latency; online also Foundry evaluators (groundedness, relevance, fluency, coherence) on English lines. Thresholds in `eval/config.yaml`, accepted scores in `eval/baseline.json`. Hard rules for the facts: 100% number match and 100% verified, no regression allowed. The AI-judged scores (1-5 scales, 16 lines: one casual and one analyst English line per moment) have a floor of 3.0 and may drop at most 15% from the baseline, because they move by about 0.5 between identical runs. Fallback (at most 25%) and first-attempt (at least 60%) rates and p95 latency (15 s online, 4 s offline) are bounded only. First online baseline (Deploy run 40102079422, gpt-5-mini, minimal reasoning): number match 1.0, verified 1.0, fallback 8.3%, first attempt 79%, p95 13.1 s, groundedness 4.81, relevance 3.94, coherence 3.94, fluency 3.31. The first online runs caught one real narration error (a momentum swing credited to the wrong team), now prevented by an explicit subject in the narrator's input and a Verifier rule that the headline names the moment's team or player. `--offline` (stub model on recorded answers) runs in CI on every push; `--online` runs after deploy.

## ADR-017: Moments that interpret, template-first cards and moment tiers (accepted, 2026-10-25)
- Why: on Azure at 10x most cards never showed (a 30 s video delay left the model 3 s; p95 is 13 s, and a late card was dropped), and only goals and red cards cleared the 0.6 importance cut, so pressing, control and tempo moments the core detected were hidden. Cards also reported what happened without the match situation that makes it matter.
- Template first, model upgrade (D4 in the viewer): `POST /api/narrate` takes `mode: "template"` and returns the Verifier-checked template line at once (no model; `template_fallback` stays false, `narration.source` says which). The viewer asks for it first, so a card is on the timeline before its moment, then asks for the model line (two requests in flight, earliest moment first), which replaces the quick line in place, on screen included. The Start form suggests `delay_s = max(30, speed x 12)` (12 s of real time for the model, at most 600 s, the core's new limit); the core logs a warning when `delay_s / speed < 8`. A moment whose card missed its slot stays in the Moments list ("not shown live, click to replay").
- Tiers: importance 0.5 and up gets the model line (lower third); 0.35 to 0.5 the template line only, as a corner card; below 0.35 the ticker only. The Moments list filters by group (goals and chances, pressing and momentum, tactics, players).
- Context facts: every insight except milestones carries `ctx_score_for`, `ctx_score_against`, `ctx_game_state` (leading, level, trailing) and `ctx_players_for` / `ctx_players_against`, from the subject team's side. They are derived from the goals and red cards added to the evidence (`F.withContext`), so Rederive still proves every fact from evidence alone (ADR-011). Detectors whose `derive` counted every evidence event in a window now filter by type (momentum: attacking actions; tempo: passes), so the extra evidence changes no existing fact. Correcting a goal now also re-issues the insights that had it as context (tested).
- Reason codes chosen with the context: `ten_men_equalise`, `late_equaliser`, `late_lead` (goals from minute 80), `ten_men_take_control`, `pressure_without_reward` (share up, no shots), `press_while_chasing`, `leaders_keep_the_ball`. The Verifier's support tokens follow the context (`ctx_game_state` supports lead and trailing claims; fewer than 11 players supports "ten men").
- New detectors (kinds already in the schema): `player_spotlight`, the pass that opened them up (a completed pass gaining at least 20 m into the final third, or one an average pass completes at most 40% of the time, followed by a non-goal shot of at least 0.1 xG by a team-mate in the same possession; codes `pass_creates_big_chance`, `creator_in_form`, `line_breaking_pass`); `tactical_shift`, team shape from event data (the mean x of a team's defensive actions in its own frame over two ten-minute spells, at least 18 actions each, moving at least 14 m; codes `sits_deep_to_protect_lead`, `team_sits_deeper`, `pushes_up_to_chase`, `team_pushes_higher`). Over 50 seeds: 4.3 spotlights and 2.1 shape shifts per match (bands 2-6 and 1-4, tested).
- Shape overlay (schema change): overlay type `shape_map` with `content.positions` (up to 11 `{player_id, x, y}` in the team's own frame) and `content.formation` (the lineup formation, only with 11 on the pitch). The core publishes one per tactical shift, like the scoreline: each player's average action position over the spell, the eleven busiest, basis `event_estimate`. The viewer draws it on the pitch in its own slot, labelled as an estimate from events.
- Narration prompt: interpret rather than report: what happened, the pattern (before against after), and why it matters given the score and the players on the pitch. The eval gate adds the top insight of each interpretive kind below the 0.6 cut (`also_kinds` in `eval/config.yaml`): 11 insights, 165 lines.

## ADR-018: Sign-in with Entra ID, an API key for services, per-user preferences (accepted, 2026-10-25)
- Why: the deployed core and agent service were open to anyone with the URL, and a viewer's settings lived in one browser. A key in a public web page is not a secret, so people sign in and only servers hold the key.
- People: the viewer signs in with Microsoft Entra ID through MSAL (`@azure/msal-browser` 5.25.0, the pinned ES module from jsDelivr, loaded only when `config.js` names an app). Two app registrations, made once by `infra/scripts/entra-apps.sh` (you run it; the deploy pipeline has no directory rights): `fd-api` exposes the scope `access_as_user`, `fd-viewer` is the single-page app. Both accept work, school and personal Microsoft accounts (`AzureADandPersonalMicrosoftAccount`, v2 tokens), so anyone with a Microsoft account can sign in. No client secret exists.
- Services: one random API key per environment in the `X-API-Key` header (the agent service and its MCP server calling the core, `follow`, the online eval). The preprovision hook keeps it (reads Key Vault, else generates 32 random bytes); Bicep stores it in Key Vault and gives it to both apps as a Container Apps secret, so they always change key in the same revision. It is masked in the Deploy logs and never written to `config.js`.
- The gate is the same in both services (`smi.api.Auth` with nimbus-jose-jwt 10.10; `smi_agents.auth` with PyJWT 2.15.1): a valid key, or an RS256 token signed with the identity platform's keys (`common/discovery/v2.0/keys`, cached), audience the `fd-api` client id, not expired, issuer `https://login.microsoftonline.com/{tid}/v2.0` for the token's own tenant, and `access_as_user` in `scp`; else 401 with `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer`. `/health` and `/openapi.yaml` stay public; CORS runs outside the gate, so preflights pass and refusals are readable. `SMI_AUTH=on` turns it on; locally it is off (tests, offline eval and local runs unchanged). Bicep turns it on only when both the key and the `fd-api` client id exist, so a deploy before the registrations exist keeps working.
- Streams: `EventSource` cannot send headers, so the viewer reads the SSE streams with `fetch` and a small tested parser (`viewer/js/sse.js`), resuming with `Last-Event-ID` after a dropped connection.
- Preferences: `GET` and `PUT /api/me/preferences` on the agent service (persona, language, favourite club and player, metric focus, Moments filters), keyed by the token's `oid`; Cosmos container `preferences` (partition `/user_id`) in Azure, in memory locally. The viewer loads them after sign-in and saves changes (debounced); the narration cache stays shared (D3). A service caller cannot save preferences (403).
- Not done: per-user rate limits, and roles (everyone signed in may start or pause the one live match).

## ADR-019: Settings you change in Azure while the demo runs (accepted, 2026-10-25)
- Why: during judging we want to change behaviour in minutes, without a code change or a 30-minute deploy: open the demo without sign-in, change the default speed, show more or fewer moments, switch the model's reasoning effort.
- One file of defaults, `config/app.yaml` (grouped, one comment per key), packaged in the core jar as `/app.yaml`. Azure App Configuration (Free tier, `appcs-fd-<token>`, local auth off) holds the same keys as `group:name`; a value there wins. 17 settings: match defaults (seed, speed, tracking, narration budget, delay bounds), moments and cards (model and template importance cuts, card minimum screen time, model narrations at once), model and narration (deployment, reasoning effort, attempts, default persona and language) and access (sign-in required, CORS origins).
- Only the core reads the store (one list call every 2 minutes, about 720 of the Free tier's 1,000 requests a day; `POST /api/config/reload` reads it at once). It checks every value against a type and range, and rules between settings (the template cut not above the model cut, the smallest delay not above the largest); a bad value is ignored, logged and listed under `problems` in the public `GET /api/config`, which also says where each value came from. The agent service polls `/api/config` every 30 s and the viewer every 60 s, so a change reaches everything within about 2.5 minutes, with no restart and no lost match.
- What applies where: the core's gate and CORS per request; the agents' model deployment and reasoning effort per model call, attempts per narration, gate and CORS per request; the viewer's tiers, card time and model slots for moments that arrive after the change, the Start form's defaults and a first-time viewer's persona and language on load. With `access:sign_in_required` false anyone may watch (people can still sign in to keep preferences; anonymous callers cannot save them).
- Seeding: the postprovision hook (`infra/hooks/seed_settings.py`, az CLI) adds the keys the store lacks and never changes one that exists, so portal edits survive redeploys; `access:cors_origins` is seeded with the viewer's address. Roles: the managed identity reads (App Configuration Data Reader); the deploy pipeline and the person in `CONFIG_EDITOR_PRINCIPAL_ID` write (Data Owner).
- New dependency: `com.azure:azure-data-appconfiguration` 1.10.2 (core). Endpoints, identities, the API key and app registrations stay infrastructure (Bicep and env vars), not settings.

## ADR-020: The viewer in React and TypeScript, proven by a parity suite (accepted, 2026-10-25)
- Why: the vanilla viewer had grown to about 1,000 lines of DOM code in one file, hard to change safely. The rewrite was built beside it in `/web` and replaced it only once an end-to-end parity suite passed.
- Stack: React 19.3 and TypeScript 5.9 with Vite 6.4; Vitest 3.2 with Testing Library and jsdom; Playwright 1.61 for end to end. MSAL (`@azure/msal-browser` 5.25.0) is now an npm dependency, split into its own chunk and loaded only when sign-in is configured. No state library and no msal-react: not needed.
- Structure: the tested pure modules (timeline and overlay queue, match facts, settings, SSE parser, sign-in) became typed modules under `src/lib`; everything that is not drawing lives in one framework-free controller (`src/broadcast.ts`: streams, timeline, narration queue with quick and model lines, preferences, settings) that publishes an immutable snapshot; React components render it through `useSyncExternalStore`. The controller takes its `fetch`, clock and storage as arguments, so it is tested against a fake core and agent service, SSE bodies included.
- New: a pre-match screen while the viewer delay runs (both teams, formations and starting elevens from `/api/tools/match_info`, and a countdown to the broadcast), replacing the blank pitch.
- Tests: Vitest (29: the ported library tests, the controller and the components) on every push; the Playwright parity suite (start at 40x with the suggested delay, busy button and toast, pre-match screen, verified cards and score, the shape map, pause and resume, re-narration in another language, filters, replay from the list, reload mid-match) against the real core jar, the agent service on the stub model and a production build, in CI on every push. Node 22 LTS locally and in CI, so the e2e suite is TypeScript too (`@types/node` 22.20.5 for its config).
- Deploy: Static Web Apps serves `web/dist`; the Deploy workflow builds it after provisioning, so the `config.js` written by the hook (endpoints, sign-in) is in the build.

## ADR-021: Moments by profile, plain or technical words, lighter overlays (accepted, 2026-10-25)
- Why: every persona saw the same moments in the same technical language (xG, PPDA, line heights), the cards covered much of the pitch, and Start stopped spinning long before anything played.
- What each persona sees (`web/src/lib/personas.ts`): casual follows the story (goals, red cards, big chances, key passes, momentum swings); the analyst sees every moment; the club fan sees every moment whose subject is their club plus the opponent's goals and red cards; player focus the moments that name the player plus goals and red cards; metric focus the moments whose facts carry the chosen metric plus goals and red cards (without a choice, each falls back to casual). Shape maps go to the analyst and metric focus only. The controller narrates only what the persona sees (fewer model calls) and re-evaluates on a change of persona or favourite; the importance tiers still apply within.
- How it is said: casual, club fan and player focus are plain; analyst and metric focus technical. The brief tells the Narrator (`plain`): everyday words, no technical statistics, no decimals, at most one simple number. A Verifier rule for plain lines rejects xG, PPDA and decimal numbers (`technical_term:*`) in every language. Template lines for plain personas keep the code's headline (all plain words) and replace the statistics with the match situation from the context facts ("Eastmere Rovers lead 2-1 with 14 minutes left.", "Northgate FC pierde 1-2 a falta de 14 minutos, y con uno menos."). Plain personas get at most one metric chip, a simple count; the analyst four, metric focus three.
- First online run (Deploy 40199853710): number match and verified still 100%, but fallback 40.6% and first attempt 36.4%, almost all from plain personas: the Narrator kept writing the decimals it was shown (192 `technical_term:decimal` rejections) and "chasing a goal" phrasing was read as a goal claim. Fix, not a looser gate: a plain persona's model sees only whole, non-technical numbers (the Verifier still checks against the full sheet), code picks its one chip, and wanting or lacking a goal ("in search of a goal", "busca el gol", "गोल की तलाश") counts as the match situation. The eval report now keeps the words of each rejected draft. Second run (Deploy 40203346554): fallback 13.3%, first attempt 64.2%, p95 15.07 s (69 ms over the bound). Its rejected drafts showed the rest were stakes phrasing ("making an equaliser much harder", "chase a 1-2 score", "fail to test the keeper", "darle la vuelta al marcador", "बराबरी से बढ़त", "टैकल जीत"), now situation phrases; replayed on that run, 65 of 86 claim-only rejections pass and real claims stay rejected (tested).
- Overlays: translucent cards with a backdrop blur, narrower, the body clamped to two lines (hover or focus shows the rest), smaller chips; the pre-match screen is translucent so the pitch shows through.
- Start: the button stays busy, reading "Starting… N s", until the first event reaches the viewer (or a failure, or the delay plus 30 s); a run started elsewhere does not make it busy.
